Concentric started with research.

We are continuing to examine the evidence as we test the model in practice.

67women across the United States

The first study

Our first research study asked women about:

  • Their primary professional challenge.
  • The support they currently had.
  • What they felt was missing.
  • What they had previously invested in.
  • And whether a small peer group could be useful.

The respondents were not representative of all professional women in the United States.

They had self-selected into research about professional support, so the findings should be understood in that context.

Four themes appeared repeatedly.

Direction uncertainty

Questioning whether the current path was still the right one.

Professional isolation

Managing professional difficulty largely alone.

Access

Lacking some of the relationships, advocacy or opportunities needed despite having professional contacts.

Recalibration

Reconsidering work after burnout, depletion or an unsustainable role.

The question that followed

Can you be well-connected and still lack the right people for a particular professional conversation?

Our research did not prove that peer Councils are the answer.

It gave us a question worth testing.

Concentric is the test.

What else informs the model

We examine:

  • Published academic and workplace research.
  • Perspectives from academics and practitioners.
  • Input from senior professional women.
  • Placement Conversations.
  • Member feedback.
  • Session observations that do not disclose private member conversations.
  • Pilot outcomes.
  • Acquisition and retention data.

We are interested in evidence that supports the model and evidence that challenges it.

How we think about outcomes

We are not assuming that success means a promotion, new job or dramatic career change.

Useful movement may be smaller.

  • A member identifies the real question.
  • An assumption changes.
  • A previously unseen option appears.
  • A plan becomes stronger.
  • A decision becomes clearer.
  • An action is taken.
  • An action is consciously declined.
  • Or a member realizes she needs more information before deciding.

We want to understand whether the Council contributed to that movement.
