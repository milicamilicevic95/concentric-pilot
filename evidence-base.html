The Evidence Base

What we know. What we are testing. What we are learning.

Concentric started with original research and is continuing to test the model in practice.

67women across the United States

The first study

We asked women what they were navigating professionally, what support they already had and what they felt was missing.

The respondents were self-selected into research about professional support, so the findings are directional rather than representative of all professional women in the United States.

Direction uncertainty

Questioning whether the current path was still the right one.

Professional isolation

Managing professional difficulty largely alone.

Access

Having contacts but still lacking some relationships, advocacy or opportunities.

Recalibration

Reconsidering work after burnout, depletion or an unsustainable role.

The question that followed

Can you be well-connected and still lack the right people for a particular professional conversation?

The first study did not prove that peer Councils are the answer.

It gave us a question worth testing.

Edition One is that live test.

What Edition One is testing

Does the room change anything?

  • Can carefully placed peers develop enough trust for useful professional conversation?
  • Does difference between members improve the conversation?
  • What makes placement work?
  • What role should facilitation play?
  • Does continuity make the Council more useful over time?
  • What changes after the conversation?

How we think about outcomes

Movement can be small.

  • A member identifies the real question.
  • An assumption changes.
  • A previously unseen option appears.
  • A plan becomes stronger.
  • A decision becomes clearer.
  • An action is taken — or consciously declined.
