You already have people to talk to.

  • Friends.
  • Colleagues.
  • Mentors.
  • Partners.
  • A network.
  • They matter.

But when something important is at stake, who gives you the “what about?”

Who questions the assumption everyone has accepted?

Who can stress-test your thinking without a stake in what you choose?

That is the role Concentric puts into the room.

A strong network can still leave a gap.

Different relationships do different jobs. None of them needs replacing. But they do not always create the conditions for candid, independent challenge.

Friend

Partner

Colleague

Mentor

Sponsor

Network

Creates access and opportunity.

The gap tends to show when

  • The question is still unfinished.
  • You know what you want and need the plan stress-tested.
  • Several options look plausible.
  • Seniority, reputation or politics make candor harder.
  • Encouragement is useful, but no longer enough.

What happens in the room?

You bring the decision, question or tension occupying your thinking.

The other five are not there to tell you what to do. They:

  • Listen carefully.
  • Ask better questions.
  • Challenge an assumption.
  • Offer another perspective.
  • Notice what you may have overlooked.
  • Give you the “what about?”

Agreement can be useful. So can disagreement.

Some sessions may surface an option you had not considered. Others may leave you less certain than when you arrived.

That can still be movement.

The point is not to leave with an answer. It is to leave having thought differently because the room existed.

Movement, not just a good conversation.

What changed because the room existed?

Progress here is not assumed to mean promotion.

It can be smaller, less linear and more specific.

  • A question gets clearer.
  • An assumption is challenged.
  • A plan survives stress-testing.
  • Action is taken — or consciously declined.

We are interested in what challenge changes, not simply whether the conversation felt good.

Something consequential is in play.

Concentric is for professionally established women at an inflection point.

Not defined by title, industry or generation.

You do not need a finished question. Some of the most useful ones are unfinished.

You might be

  • Considering a role or career change.
  • Facing redundancy, re-entry or a new chapter.
  • Leaving a successful path for something different.
  • Clear on the destination, uncertain about the route.
  • Noticing that something no longer fits.
