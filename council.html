The Council

Five other minds on the question.

A Concentric Council brings together six professionally established women for one 90-minute online conversation each month.

Each member brings real professional decisions, questions and situations.

The Council helps her examine them from more than one perspective.

The format

What you are joining.

Six members

Each Council has six women.

That is small enough for everyone to contribute and large enough to bring different experiences and perspectives into the conversation.

One meeting a month

Councils meet online for 90 minutes.

The same women

You return to the same Council each month.

That means you do not have to explain your entire professional history every time you want to discuss something.

Members build an understanding of one another’s context over time.

A neutral facilitator

Every meeting has a facilitator.

Her job is to manage the structure, time and participation.

She does not coach members, provide professional advice or lead members toward a particular decision.

Shared principles

Members agree to clear expectations around confidentiality, participation and how they engage with one another.

The conversation

What actually happens during the 90 minutes?

Members bring what is currently occupying their professional thinking.

The conversation is structured so there is time to examine those issues properly.

When another member brings something to the Council, your role is not to solve it for her.

  • You listen.
  • You ask questions.
  • You share relevant experience.
  • You point out something she may not have considered.
  • You challenge an assumption when it needs challenging.
  • You help her examine the decision from another angle.

When it is your turn, the other five do the same for you.

Advice may be useful. Questions often are too.

The decision remains yours.

What you can bring

It does not need to be dramatic.

  • Should I take this role?
  • Am I solving the wrong problem?
  • Do I actually want the promotion I have been working toward?
  • How should I approach this conversation?
  • Is this plan realistic?
  • What am I missing?
  • Should I leave?
  • Should I stay?
  • Why have I stopped progressing?
  • How do I make this change without starting again?
  • What would someone outside my company see here?
  • Or simply: I know something needs to change. I am not yet sure what.

What it is not

Concentric is peer advisory.

Not

Coaching

There is no coach providing the answer or setting your goals.

Not

Networking

You are not meeting a new group of people every month or collecting contacts.

Not

Group therapy

Councils may discuss difficult professional experiences, but Concentric is not a mental health service.

Not

A mastermind

There is no guru, prescribed business model or expectation that every member is pursuing the same outcome.

It is peer advisory.

Six women bringing experience, questions and different perspectives to one another’s professional decisions.

Continuity

The same room gets more useful over time.

A one-off conversation can be valuable.

Continuity adds something different.

When the same women return each month, they remember the decision you were considering.

They know which assumptions you were making.

They can ask what happened.

And you can do the same for them.

The Council develops context that a room of strangers cannot have.

Bring the question while it is still open.
